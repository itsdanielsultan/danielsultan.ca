How I built my CIRE course.

An independent, free and open-source study resource by Daniel Sultan.

I built the first version over three weeks. The goal was to turn a technical syllabus into a course someone could follow from the beginning, with explanations and practice in the same place.

Try the courseRead the source
The starting point

Explain the idea before the rule.

The official material I started with was spread across three PDFs. It was useful for identifying what to study, but the terminology could be difficult without a foundation in finance.

I organized the course around that missing foundation. The first lesson asks “What is investing?” Later lessons build toward products, regulation and the responsibilities of investment professionals.

The site is a study aid for the Canadian Investment Regulatory Exam, not an official course, professional credential or guarantee of exam results.

Lessons, flashcards and a searchable glossary help introduce the material. Short drills and a 100-question mock exam give learners another way to work through it, then review what they missed.

Bookmarks and progress stay in the learner’s browser. There is no account or fee, and the source is open for inspection. The mock is a practice tool, not an exact replica of the official exam.

Design decisions

Keep the next step clear.

The lesson sequence matters as much as the individual explanations. Instead of starting with a quiz, the course gives a learner a route through the concepts, then places practice and review alongside it.

The interface uses a restrained visual system, with strong typography, clear navigation and light and dark themes. Lessons, flashcards and drills each have a distinct job without feeling like separate products.

Make the work inspectable.

Publishing the source lets people look at the questions and implementation, report problems and suggest improvements. Explanations and practice material still need judgement and checking against current official requirements.

What came next

A second learning tool: IFC Prep.

In October I published a separate companion for the Investment Funds in Canada course. It brings together 54 lessons, workshops, flashcards and original practice questions, with offline and printable options.

It follows the same principle: make the material easier to work through and keep progress with the learner. IFC Prep is independent, not CSI-endorsed, and does not replace the purchased course.
