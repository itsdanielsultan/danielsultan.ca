Future Mississauga.

Making the city’s development easier to understand through an independent 3D atlas.

I bring public data, development drawings and my own modelling into one place, with sources and estimates people can inspect.

Seeing the city together.

A development rendering shows one site. I wanted to understand how new buildings and transit fit into the wider city. The atlas brings that context together through neighbourhood views, an interactive explorer and comparisons from the same viewpoint.

From mapped form to reference-based model.

Architectural references helped me refine 1355 Winding Trail. The matched views make the changes visible: I added the tower, glazed front bays and lower green roofs to the original mapped building.

Architectural references were shared by Kleinfeldt Mychajlowycz Architects. The City footprint provides the starting scale and shape. Tower height, roof levels and unseen surfaces remain estimates; the ground is a display base, not a survey.

Building with the sources.

I check footprints, addresses and orientation against available drawings and photographs, then review the model beside roads, terrain and neighbouring buildings. I keep existing context, recorded construction and future proposals distinct.

This is the part of the work that connects to my wider interests: finding the useful information, checking it, and presenting it clearly enough for another person to explore.

Sources and limitations.

Future Mississauga is independent work. Some forms are schematic, hidden details are estimated and source dates vary. A future scenario is not a forecast: proposals may change or never be built.

The downtown image is my October 23, 2026 render. Gallery images and model releases carry their own dates.
